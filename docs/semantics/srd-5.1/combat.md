# Combat

<a id="srd51-core-order-of-combat"></a>
## Order of combat

`srd51.core.order-of-combat`

To begin an encounter, the GM first determines surprise, then establishes the positions of all combatants from their marching order or declared positions and the opposition's location. Roll initiative and take one turn per participant in that order. A round represents about six seconds and ends after every participant has taken their turn. Repeat turns in the same order for each further round until combat stops. [SRD p. 90, The Order of Combat and Combat Step by Step](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-090.md)

<a id="srd51-core-surprise"></a>
## Surprise

`srd51.core.surprise`

The GM determines which creatures are surprised. When neither side uses stealth, they notice each other automatically. Otherwise, compare hiding creatures' Dexterity (Stealth) checks against each opponent's [passive Wisdom (Perception)](abilities.md#srd51-core-passive-check). A creature that notices no threat is surprised at the start of combat. Resolve this separately for each creature. A surprised creature cannot move or take an action on its first turn and cannot react until that turn ends. It still has an initiative place and takes its first turn under those restrictions. After that turn ends it can react if otherwise eligible. [SRD p. 90, Surprise and Initiative](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-090.md)

<a id="srd51-core-initiative"></a>
## Initiative

`srd51.core.initiative`

When combat begins each participant makes a [Dexterity check](abilities.md#srd51-core-ability-check). The GM rolls once for a group of identical creatures, and its members act at the same initiative. Rank totals from highest to lowest and retain that order for later rounds. In a tie, the players decide among their characters and the GM decides among GM-controlled creatures. The GM can decide a mixed monster and player character tie. The GM may instead have tied characters and monsters each roll a d20 and rank the highest first. [SRD p. 90, Initiative](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-090.md)

<a id="srd51-core-turn"></a>
## Your turn

`srd51.core.turn`

On your turn you can move up to your speed and take one action. You choose the order and can decline movement, the action, or both. See [combat movement](#srd51-core-combat-movement) for dividing movement and [actions in combat](#srd51-core-actions-in-combat) for the common choices. Features and abilities can grant additional action options. [SRD p. 90, Your Turn](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-090.md)

<a id="srd51-core-bonus-action"></a>
### Bonus actions

`srd51.core.bonus-action`

Take a bonus action only if a feature, spell, or other rule grants a specific bonus action. Use at most one on your turn and select among options you qualify for. You choose its timing on the turn subject to its specified timing and prerequisites. Anything that prevents taking actions also prevents taking a bonus action. [SRD pp. 90–91, Bonus Actions](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-090.md) · [SRD p. 91, Bonus Actions continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md)

<a id="srd51-core-other-activity-on-your-turn"></a>
### Other activity on your turn

`srd51.core.other-activity-on-your-turn`

You can communicate briefly by speech or gesture as your abilities allow, without spending your action or movement. During your move or action you can interact with one object or environmental feature for free. A second interaction uses your action. An object can require an action for its use, and the GM can require one when an interaction presents unusual difficulty or needs care. Simple manipulations such as drawing or putting away a weapon, passing an item, or opening an ordinary door can qualify for the free interaction. Opening a jammed door may instead require an action. [SRD p. 91, Other Activity on Your Turn](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md) · [SRD p. 92, Interacting with Objects Around You](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-092.md)

<a id="srd51-core-reaction"></a>
### Reactions

`srd51.core.reaction`

Track reaction availability separately from the action taken on a turn. Its use requires an applicable trigger and can occur on any participant's turn. Respond immediately at that trigger unless the granting rule specifies a different timing. After resolving one, restore availability when your own next turn begins. If it paused another creature's turn, resume that turn immediately after resolution. Timing comes from the granting rule. [Ready](#srd51-core-ready) resolves after the trigger finishes. An opportunity attack occurs before the target crosses out of reach. [SRD p. 91, Reactions](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md) · [SRD p. 93, Ready](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD p. 95, Opportunity Attacks](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md)

<a id="srd51-core-combat-movement"></a>
## Movement and position

`srd51.core.combat-movement`

Spend up to your speed on movement during your turn. Walking, jumping, swimming, and climbing can make up all or part of the move. Deduct the movement spent for each segment, using [special movement](exploration.md#srd51-core-special-movement) and terrain costs as applicable. You can split movement before and after an action. When an action includes multiple weapon attacks, you can also move between those attacks. [SRD p. 91, Movement and Position and Breaking Up Your Move](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md)

If you have more than one speed, you can switch back and forth. Each switch subtracts the distance already moved from the newly selected speed. A result of 0 or less permits no further movement using that speed on the current move. This does not add your listed speeds together. For example, with a speed of 25 feet and fly speed of 40, after flying 15 feet you can walk up to 10 feet. If you then switch back to flying after walking those 10 feet, you have 15 feet of flight left. [SRD p. 91, Using Different Speeds](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md)

**Project interpretation.** When switching speeds, deduct the movement budget already spent, including extra terrain and other movement costs. The switching paragraph says to subtract distance already moved, which could mean physical displacement alone. The movement and terrain provisions require deductions for each segment, so carrying the actual costs forward preserves those deductions across a switch. For example, crossing 10 feet of difficult terrain on foot spends 20 feet. Switching to a fly speed of 40 then permits 20 more feet of flight under this reading. [SRD p. 91, Movement and Position, Using Different Speeds, and Difficult Terrain](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md)

<a id="srd51-core-combat-difficult-terrain"></a>
### Difficult terrain

`srd51.core.combat-difficult-terrain`

Every foot crossed in difficult terrain costs one extra foot of movement, regardless of how many features of a location make it difficult. Another creature's space counts, whether the creature is friendly or hostile. Rubble, undergrowth, steep steps, furniture, snow, or shallow bogs can also count. [SRD p. 91, Difficult Terrain](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md)

<a id="srd51-core-being-prone"></a>
### Being prone

`srd51.core.being-prone`

You can drop [prone](conditions.md#srd51-condition-prone) without spending movement. Standing up ends Prone and costs movement equal to half your speed. You must have enough movement remaining and a speed greater than 0. While prone you must crawl or use magic such as teleportation to move. Crawling adds one foot of cost per foot traveled, so crawling through difficult terrain costs three feet of movement per foot traveled. [SRD pp. 91–92, Being Prone](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md) · [SRD p. 92, Being Prone continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-092.md)

<a id="srd51-core-moving-around-other-creatures"></a>
### Moving around other creatures

`srd51.core.moving-around-other-creatures`

You may move through a nonhostile creature's space. You may move through a hostile creature's space only if it is at least two size categories larger or smaller than you. Another creature's space costs movement as difficult terrain, and you cannot willingly end your move in it. Leaving a hostile creature's reach can provoke an [opportunity attack](#srd51-core-opportunity-attack). [SRD p. 92, Moving Around Other Creatures](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-092.md) · [SRD p. 95, Opportunity Attacks](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md)

<a id="srd51-core-flying-movement"></a>
### Flying movement

`srd51.core.flying-movement`

A flying creature falls if knocked prone, reduced to speed 0, or otherwise deprived of movement, unless it can hover or is held aloft by magic. Use [falling](exploration.md#srd51-core-falling) for damage and landing. [SRD p. 92, Flying Movement](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-092.md) · [SRD p. 86, Falling](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

<a id="srd51-core-creature-size"></a>
### Creature size

`srd51.core.creature-size`

Size determines the square of space a creature controls in combat, distinct from its physical dimensions. Tiny uses 2.5 feet on a side. Small and Medium each use 5 feet. Large uses 10, Huge 15, and Gargantuan 20 feet or more. Objects sometimes use the same categories. The controlled space limits passage and how many creatures fit around a target. With Medium creatures, eight fit around another Medium creature within 5 feet. Five Large creatures around a Medium or smaller creature leave little room for others, and a Gargantuan creature can have as many as twenty Medium creatures surround it. [SRD p. 92, Creature Size and Space](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-092.md)

<a id="srd51-core-squeezing"></a>
### Squeezing

`srd51.core.squeezing`

A creature may squeeze through a space large enough for the next smaller size. While squeezing, each foot traveled there costs one extra foot of movement. The creature has disadvantage on attack rolls and Dexterity saves, and attack rolls against it have advantage. For example, a Large creature can squeeze through a 5-foot passage. [SRD p. 92, Squeezing into a Smaller Space](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-092.md)

**Acceptance cases.** At speed 30 feet with 15 feet of movement left, a prone creature can stand and has no movement left afterward. With 14 feet left, it cannot stand. A Medium creature may pass through a hostile Huge creature's space, but a hostile Large creature is only one size larger and blocks passage under this rule. A prone flying creature with hover does not fall solely because it is prone. [SRD p. 92, Being Prone continuation, Moving Around Other Creatures, Flying Movement, and Creature Size](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-092.md)

<a id="srd51-core-actions-in-combat"></a>
## Actions in combat

`srd51.core.actions-in-combat`

Use your action for a general action, an action supplied by your features or stat block, or an improvised action. For an improvised action, the GM determines whether it is possible and what roll, if any, resolves it. [SRD p. 93, Actions in Combat](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md)

<a id="srd51-core-attack-action"></a>
### Attack action

`srd51.core.attack-action`

Spend your action to make one melee or ranged [attack](#srd51-core-attack). A feature such as Extra Attack can increase the number of attacks available within this action. Certain special attacks can replace one attack within it. A separate rule can grant an individual attack without using the Attack action. [SRD p. 93, Attack](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD pp. 95–96, Opportunity Attacks, Grappling, and Shoving a Creature](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md) · [SRD p. 96, Shoving a Creature](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md)

<a id="srd51-core-cast-a-spell-action"></a>
### Cast a Spell action

`srd51.core.cast-a-spell-action`

When casting a spell with a casting time of one action in combat, use your action to [cast the spell](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-cast-spell). Spells with other casting times use the timing and cost of their entries. [SRD p. 93, Cast a Spell](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD p. 101, Casting Time](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-101.md)

<a id="srd51-core-dash"></a>
### Dash

`srd51.core.dash`

Spend your action to gain extra movement on the current turn equal to your speed after modifiers. Increases or decreases to speed change the extra movement by the same amount. A speed of 20 feet after modifications yields 20 feet of extra movement, for a total of 40 feet before movement costs. [SRD p. 93, Dash](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md)

<a id="srd51-core-disengage"></a>
### Disengage

`srd51.core.disengage`

After spending your action to Disengage, your movement for the rest of that turn does not provoke opportunity attacks. [SRD p. 93, Disengage](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md)

<a id="srd51-core-dodge"></a>
### Dodge

`srd51.core.dodge`

Spend your action to Dodge. Until the start of your next turn, attacks against you have disadvantage when you can see the attacker, and you have advantage on Dexterity saves. Lose the benefit if you become [incapacitated](conditions.md#srd51-condition-incapacitated) or your speed drops to 0. [SRD p. 93, Dodge](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md)

<a id="srd51-core-help"></a>
### Help

`srd51.core.help`

For task assistance, use your action to help another creature with an eligible task under [Working Together](abilities.md#srd51-core-working-together). It gains advantage on its next ability check for that task if made before your next turn starts. Alternatively, aid a friendly creature's attack by distracting or otherwise acting against a target within 5 feet of you. If the ally attacks that target before your next turn, its first attack roll against the target has advantage. The 5-foot requirement measures from the helper to the target. [SRD p. 93, Help](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD p. 79, Working Together](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-079.md)

<a id="srd51-core-hide"></a>
### Hide

`srd51.core.hide`

Use your action to attempt [hiding](abilities.md#srd51-core-hiding) with Dexterity (Stealth). Successful hiding can give the [benefits of attacking unseen](#srd51-core-unseen-attackers-and-targets). [SRD p. 93, Hide](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD p. 94, Unseen Attackers and Targets](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md)

<a id="srd51-core-ready"></a>
### Ready

`srd51.core.ready`

Use your action on your turn to name a perceivable trigger and choose either an action to perform when it happens or movement up to your speed. When that trigger finishes before your next turn starts, choose whether to ignore it or use your [reaction](#srd51-core-reaction) immediately after it. You need an available reaction at that time. The permission expires at the start of your next turn. An event you cannot perceive is an invalid trigger. The reaction follows the trigger, so it cannot retroactively prevent an event that has already completed. [SRD p. 93, Ready](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md)

To Ready a spell, choose one with a casting time of exactly 1 action. You [cast it normally](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-cast-spell) as part of taking Ready and hold its energy. Release that energy using your reaction when the trigger finishes. Holding any readied spell requires [concentration](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-concentration), even for a spell whose ordinary duration is instantaneous. If you lose that concentration before releasing the energy, the spell dissipates without an effect. [SRD p. 93, Ready](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD p. 94, Ready continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md)

**Project interpretation.** At the start of your next turn the unused held energy dissipates and its concentration obligation ends, since the spell has no remaining release window. The source gives the release deadline and specifies dissipation on lost concentration, but gives no further use or duration for unused energy. [SRD pp. 93–94, Ready](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD p. 94, Ready continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md)

**Project interpretation.** For a readied spell, pay and validate casting requirements when taking Ready. Choose the targets or area origin and evaluate [range](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-spell-range), [clear path](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-clear-path-to-target), and any sight requirement when releasing the energy. The Ready declaration chooses a spell and a trigger, and the release is when an effect can first reach anything. An alternative reading fixes the target during the initial normal casting. The cited passages do not expressly fix when to choose targets for Ready. An active duration begins at release. The hold consumes concentration before release independently of that duration. [SRD pp. 93–94, Ready](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD p. 94, Ready continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md) · [SRD p. 101, Range](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-101.md) · [SRD p. 102, Targets and Duration](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-102.md)

**Derived conclusion.** Since the spell is cast when readied, spend its required [slot](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-spell-slots) and provide its [components](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-spell-components) at that casting. Dissipation or expiry of the reaction window gives no slot or consumed component refund. The no-slot loss provision for a failed [long casting](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-longer-casting-times) concerns a casting that has yet to finish. Ready permits a casting of exactly 1 action followed by a separate hold. [SRD pp. 93–94, Ready](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD p. 94, Ready continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md) · [SRD p. 100, Spell Slots](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-100.md) · [SRD pp. 101–102, Longer Casting Times and Components](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-101.md) · [SRD p. 102, Components continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-102.md)

**Acceptance cases.** A wizard concentrating on an ongoing spell Readies a one-action spell to release just after the portcullis rises. The previous spell ends when the wizard begins holding the new spell. If the trigger finishes first while concentration and the reaction are available, the wizard can release the new spell or ignore the trigger. If the wizard takes 24 damage from one source before that trigger, a DC 12 Constitution save is needed to retain the held spell. If the wizard becomes [incapacitated](conditions.md#srd51-condition-incapacitated) before release, the held spell dissipates without a save and the wizard cannot react. Removing the condition later does not restore the dissipated spell. A bonus-action spell cannot be Readied. [SRD p. 93, Ready](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-093.md) · [SRD p. 94, Ready continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md) · [SRD p. 102, Concentration](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-102.md) · [SRD p. 358, Incapacitated](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-358.md)

<a id="srd51-core-search"></a>
### Search

`srd51.core.search`

Use your action to look for something. Depending on the search, the GM can call for Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (Investigation). [SRD p. 94, Search](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md)

<a id="srd51-core-use-an-object"></a>
### Use an Object

`srd51.core.use-an-object`

Use this action when an object requires an action for its use or when you need another object interaction on the turn beyond the ordinary free interaction. A specific magic item can prescribe its own activation action. [SRD p. 94, Use an Object](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md) · [SRD p. 91, Other Activity on Your Turn](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md)

<a id="srd51-core-attack"></a>
## Making an attack

`srd51.core.attack`

Choose a creature, object, or location within the attack's range. The GM determines cover, advantage, and disadvantage. Apply relevant modifiers from spells, features, or other effects. For an ordinary attack, resolve the [attack roll](#srd51-core-attack-roll). On a hit, roll and apply damage unless the attack specifies another resolution, and apply any effects it specifies in addition to or instead of damage. Making an attack roll always counts as making an attack. A specific rule can also expressly classify an effect as an attack even when it uses a different resolution, as for [grappling](#srd51-core-grappling) and [shoving](#srd51-core-shoving). [SRD p. 94, Making an Attack](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md) · [SRD pp. 95–96, Grappling and Shoving a Creature](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md) · [SRD p. 96, Shoving a Creature](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md)

<a id="srd51-core-attack-roll"></a>
### Attack rolls

`srd51.core.attack-roll`

Roll a d20 with applicable advantage or disadvantage, then add the attack modifiers. Meet or exceed the target's Armor Class to hit. Character creation establishes a character's AC, subject to the armor and other rules that change it. Monsters supply AC and attack modifiers in their stat blocks. For character melee weapon attacks use Strength by default, and for ranged weapon attacks use Dexterity by default. Finesse and thrown weapon properties can override that choice. Add proficiency bonus for an attack with a weapon in which you are proficient and for a spell attack. Spell attacks use the relevant [spellcasting ability](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-spell-attack-roll). [SRD p. 94, Attack Rolls and Modifiers to the Roll](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md)

A selected d20 result of 1 misses, and a selected result of 20 hits and is a [critical hit](#srd51-core-critical-hits), regardless of modifiers or AC. Apply targeting eligibility first. A natural 20 does not make an out-of-range target eligible or hit a creature absent from a guessed location. [SRD p. 94, Making an Attack, Rolling 1 or 20, and Unseen Attackers and Targets](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md)

<a id="srd51-core-unseen-attackers-and-targets"></a>
### Unseen attackers and targets

`srd51.core.unseen-attackers-and-targets`

An attack has disadvantage against a target you cannot see, even if you hear it or aim at a guessed location. If it is not at the chosen location, the attack automatically misses. The GM typically reports only the miss so the guess is not confirmed or disproved. If the target cannot see you, you have advantage on attacks against it. A hidden attacker, both unseen and unheard, reveals their location once the attack hits or misses. If neither combatant can see the other, the respective advantage and disadvantage cancel. [SRD p. 94, Unseen Attackers and Targets](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md) · [SRD p. 76, Advantage and Disadvantage](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-076.md)

<a id="srd51-core-ranged-attacks"></a>
### Ranged attacks

`srd51.core.ranged-attacks`

With a single range, you can attack targets no farther than that limit. With two ranges, the smaller is normal range and the larger is long range. Beyond normal range and up to long range the roll has disadvantage. Beyond long range the target is ineligible. These limits apply to weapon, spell, and other ranged attacks as their entries specify. [SRD p. 95, Ranged Attacks and Range](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md)

<a id="srd51-core-ranged-attacks-in-close-combat"></a>
### Ranged attacks in close combat

`srd51.core.ranged-attacks-in-close-combat`

Apply disadvantage on a ranged attack roll if any hostile creature within 5 feet can see you and is not [incapacitated](conditions.md#srd51-condition-incapacitated). The nearby creature need not be the target. This applies to ranged attacks from weapons, spells, and other means. [SRD p. 95, Ranged Attacks in Close Combat](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md)

<a id="srd51-core-melee-attacks"></a>
### Melee attacks

`srd51.core.melee-attacks`

A melee attack targets something within the attacker's reach, ordinarily 5 feet. A creature's entry, weapon, or feature can specify a different reach. Melee attacks can use a weapon, a body part, or a spell as applicable. [SRD p. 95, Melee Attacks](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md)

<a id="srd51-core-unarmed-strike"></a>
### Unarmed strike

`srd51.core.unarmed-strike`

Use a forceful body blow to make a melee weapon attack without wielding a weapon. You are proficient with your unarmed strikes. A hit deals bludgeoning damage equal to 1 plus your Strength modifier, subject to the ordinary minimum of zero damage. The attack does not make your body part a weapon for weapon requirements. [SRD p. 95, Melee Attacks](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md) · [SRD p. 96, Damage Rolls](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md)

<a id="srd51-core-opportunity-attack"></a>
### Opportunity attacks

`srd51.core.opportunity-attack`

If a hostile creature you can see moves out of your reach, you can use an available reaction to make one melee attack against it just before it leaves. Movement after [Disengage](#srd51-core-disengage) during the same turn does not provoke. Teleportation and displacement that uses none of the creature's movement, action, or reaction also do not provoke. For example, a visible enemy who walks away provokes if otherwise eligible, while one hurled out of reach by a blast or falling past under gravity does not. If a creature must use its reaction to leave, involuntariness alone does not supply the displacement exception. [SRD p. 95, Opportunity Attacks](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md)

<a id="srd51-core-two-weapon-fighting"></a>
### Two-weapon fighting

`srd51.core.two-weapon-fighting`

When you attack as part of the Attack action with a light melee weapon held in one hand, you may spend your bonus action to attack with a different light melee weapon held in the other hand. Omit the ability modifier from the bonus attack's damage if it is zero or positive. Retain a negative modifier. If either weapon has the thrown property, it can be thrown for its respective attack instead of used in melee. [SRD p. 95, Two-Weapon Fighting](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md)

<a id="srd51-core-grappling"></a>
### Grappling

`srd51.core.grappling`

Replace one attack of your Attack action with a special melee attack against a creature within reach and at most one size category larger than you. You need at least one free hand. Make Strength (Athletics) contested by the target's choice of Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics). Winning imposes [Grappled](conditions.md#srd51-condition-grappled). A tie or loss leaves the target free of this grapple. You can release the target at any time without an action. To escape, the target spends its action and must win with its choice of Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) contested by your Strength (Athletics). A tie leaves the existing grapple in place. [SRD p. 95, Grappling and Escaping a Grapple](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md) · [SRD p. 77, Contests](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-077.md)

When you move, you may drag or carry the grappled creature with you. This halves your speed unless the target is at least two size categories smaller. Other ending conditions are in the condition definition. [SRD p. 96, Moving a Grappled Creature](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md) · [SRD p. 358, Grappled](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-358.md)

<a id="srd51-core-shoving"></a>
### Shoving a creature

`srd51.core.shoving`

Replace one attack of your Attack action with a special melee attack against a creature within reach and at most one size category larger. Make Strength (Athletics) contested by the target's choice of Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics). On a win, choose to knock it [prone](conditions.md#srd51-condition-prone) or push it 5 feet away from you. A tie or loss does neither. The GM can use the grapple and shove contests as models for other opposed combat actions. [SRD p. 96, Contests in Combat and Shoving a Creature](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md) · [SRD p. 77, Contests](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-077.md)

<a id="srd51-core-cover"></a>
## Cover

`srd51.core.cover`

Cover applies only if the effect originates across an obstacle from the target. Use the most protective applicable degree when multiple obstacles give cover. Half cover from an obstacle blocking at least half the body grants +2 AC and Dexterity saves. Three-quarters cover from an obstacle blocking about three quarters grants +5 to both. Total cover from complete obstruction prevents direct attack or spell targeting. Some spells can reach the target as part of an [area of effect](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-spell-area) if the propagation rule allows it. Creatures as well as objects can be obstacles. For example, half cover from an intervening creature and three-quarters cover from an intervening wall give +5, not +7. [SRD p. 96, Cover](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md)

<a id="srd51-core-hit-points"></a>
## Hit points

`srd51.core.hit-points`

Track current hit points from 0 up to the creature's hit point maximum. Damage reduces current hit points after applying the relevant damage rules and any temporary hit point buffer. Losing hit points above 0 does not by itself change capabilities. Other rules can respond to taking damage or to specific hit point thresholds. Use [dropping to 0](#srd51-core-dropping-to-zero-hit-points) when damage brings the creature to 0. [SRD p. 96, Hit Points](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md) · [SRD pp. 98–99, Temporary Hit Points](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md) · [SRD p. 99, Temporary Hit Points continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-099.md)

<a id="srd51-core-damage-roll"></a>
## Damage rolls

`srd51.core.damage-roll`

Start with the effect's damage expression. For a weapon hit, carry over the attack's ability modifier unless a specific rule changes that contribution, as two-weapon fighting can. For a spell, use the spell's stated modifiers. Apply relevant bonuses or penalties from other effects, with a minimum damage result of 0. For one effect damaging multiple targets simultaneously, share a single roll. Resolve that result separately for each recipient using its saves and damage adjustments. A shared total of 14 can deal the full 14 on a failed save and 7 on a successful half-damage save. [SRD p. 96, Damage Rolls](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md) · [SRD p. 95, Two-Weapon Fighting](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-095.md) · [SRD p. 103, Saving Throws](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-103.md)

**Derived conclusion.** A resolved damage amount of 0 does not trigger effects keyed to taking damage, such as a concentration save or a death save failure at 0 hit points. An attack can still hit and trigger an effect that depends only on hitting. [SRD p. 94, Making an Attack](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-094.md) · [SRD p. 96, Damage Rolls](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md) · [SRD p. 98, Damage at 0 Hit Points](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md) · [SRD p. 102, Concentration](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-102.md)

<a id="srd51-core-periodic-object-timing"></a>
### Periodic effects on objects

`srd51.core.periodic-object-timing`

**Project interpretation.** When an effect expressly affects an object but schedules a recurring outcome on the affected target's turn, keep the effect active and give it a round-based clock. In initiative, first resolve it on the matching edge of the applying creature's next turn and repeat at that initiative count and edge every round. Keep that count if the applier leaves the encounter. If there is no applying creature or no initiative, use six-second intervals after application. A separate turn granted to an object or a different timing named by the effect takes precedence. This preserves both the listed target and the recurring effect. Another reading treats the occurrence as unavailable until the object is given its own turn. For example, a Magmin's Touch that ignites a chair deals the first recurring damage at the end of the magmin's next turn. [SRD p. 90, The Order of Combat](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-090.md) · [SRD p. 203, Objects](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-203.md) · [SRD p. 307, Fire Elemental, Touch](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-307.md) · [SRD p. 329, Magmin, Touch](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-329.md) · [SRD p. 66, Alchemist's Fire](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-066.md)

<a id="srd51-core-critical-hits"></a>
### Critical hits

`srd51.core.critical-hits`

On a critical hit, roll each damage die of the attack twice, including additional damage dice from a feature such as Sneak Attack. Apply any fixed modifiers once as usual. You can roll all the dice together. A critical attack dealing 1d8 + 2d6 + 3 on an ordinary hit deals 2d8 + 4d6 + 3. [SRD pp. 96–97, Critical Hits](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-096.md) · [SRD p. 97, Critical Hits continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-097.md)

<a id="srd51-core-damage-types"></a>
### Damage types

`srd51.core.damage-types`

Damage types classify an effect and can be used by resistance, vulnerability, and other rules. The type has no independent damage calculation. For new effects the GM can use these categories. Acid corrodes. Bludgeoning is impact or crushing. Cold is freezing injury. Fire burns. Force is focused magical energy. Lightning is electrical discharge. Necrotic damage withers matter or vitality. Piercing punctures. Poison comes from toxic substances, including venom. Psychic damage harms through mental power. Radiant damage sears by divine or spiritual energy. Slashing cuts. Thunder comes from concussive sound. [SRD p. 97, Damage Types](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-097.md)

<a id="srd51-core-damage-resistance-vulnerability"></a>
### Damage resistance and vulnerability

`srd51.core.damage-resistance-vulnerability`

For damage of a type to which the target has resistance, halve that damage. For vulnerability, double it. Apply both after all other modifiers to damage, resistance first and then vulnerability. Multiple applicable resistances of the same type count as one resistance, and multiple vulnerabilities of the same type count as one vulnerability. An applicable modifier may qualify only particular sources, such as nonmagical fire. For example, a creature with both fire resistance and resistance to damage from nonmagical sources still halves nonmagical fire damage only once. [SRD p. 97, Damage Resistance and Vulnerability](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-097.md)

**Project interpretation.** When a damage division produces a fraction, use the lower whole number for damage before the next adjustment. These damage passages specify halving without a rounding direction. The lower whole result preserves integer hit point changes, uses the floor explicitly shown for a different calculation on p. 76, and makes the required resistance-before-vulnerability order meaningful for odd damage. Keeping the fraction through the whole calculation is another reading. Under this interpretation, 15 damage with resistance and vulnerability becomes 7 and then 14. [SRD p. 97, Damage Resistance and Vulnerability](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-097.md) · [SRD p. 76, Ability Scores and Modifiers](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-076.md)

<a id="srd51-core-healing"></a>
## Healing

`srd51.core.healing`

Add hit points regained from a healing effect or rest to current hit points and discard any gain above the current maximum. Ordinary healing works on living creatures within the effect's eligibility. A dead creature cannot regain hit points until magic restores it to life. A creature at 9 of 13 hit points receiving 7 healing gains 4. [SRD p. 97, Healing](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-097.md)

<a id="srd51-core-dropping-to-zero-hit-points"></a>
## Dropping to 0 hit points

`srd51.core.dropping-to-zero-hit-points`

When damage brings a creature to 0 hit points, compare any remaining damage with its hit point maximum. If the remainder meets or exceeds the maximum, the creature dies immediately. Otherwise it becomes [unconscious](conditions.md#srd51-condition-unconscious). That unconsciousness ends when the creature regains any hit points. Monster adjudication and melee knockouts can change the outcome. With a maximum of 16 and 7 current hit points, 23 damage causes instant death because 16 remains. With 22 damage, 15 remains and the creature instead falls unconscious under the ordinary rule. [SRD pp. 97–98, Dropping to 0 Hit Points, Instant Death, and Falling Unconscious](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-097.md) · [SRD p. 98, Instant Death and Falling Unconscious](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md)

**Project interpretation.** If a non-damaging effect brings a living player character to 0 hit points, apply the ordinary unconscious and unstable state with death saves at the usual time unless the effect states a different result. The section introduces the 0-hit-point outcome generally, while its unconsciousness example rule explicitly describes damage. An alternative is to limit that rule to damage and require the reducing effect to supply all non-damage consequences. Under this reading, a non-damage reduction triggers neither damage overflow nor other effects keyed to damage. Keep the GM's normal [monster death decision](#srd51-core-monsters-and-death). Specific effects such as [suffocation](exploration.md#srd51-core-suffocating) can supply additional consequences and limits. [SRD pp. 97–98, Dropping to 0 Hit Points and Falling Unconscious](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-097.md) · [SRD p. 98, Falling Unconscious, Death Saving Throws, and Monsters and Death](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md) · [SRD p. 86, Suffocating](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

<a id="srd51-core-death-saving-throws"></a>
### Death saving throws

`srd51.core.death-saving-throws`

At the start of each turn with 0 hit points while alive and unstable, roll a d20 for a death saving throw. It uses no ability modifier, though a feature or spell can affect the save. A result of 10 or more succeeds and a lower result fails. The third success stabilizes the creature. The third failure kills it. Track the counts independently without requiring consecutive results. Regaining any hit points or becoming stable resets both counts to zero. A natural 1 gives two failures and a natural 20 restores 1 hit point, subject to rules that prevent healing. [SRD p. 98, Death Saving Throws and Rolling 1 or 20](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md) · [SRD p. 86, Suffocating](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

Taking damage while already at 0 inflicts one failure, or two if the damage comes from a critical hit. Damage that equals or exceeds the creature's hit point maximum at 0 causes instant death. Damage also ends stability, if present. For example, a living creature at 0 with one failure dies upon taking damage from a critical hit, unless a specific rule prevents the result. [SRD p. 98, Damage at 0 Hit Points and Stabilizing a Creature](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md)

<a id="srd51-core-stabilizing-a-creature"></a>
### Stabilizing a creature

`srd51.core.stabilizing-a-creature`

Spend your action to give first aid to an unconscious creature at 0 hit points. A successful DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check stabilizes it. A stable creature at 0 remains unconscious but skips death saving throws. If it takes damage, it loses stability and resumes death saves at the usual time if it survives the damage. If it remains stable and is not healed, it regains 1 hit point after 1d4 hours. A rule such as [suffocating](exploration.md#srd51-core-suffocating) can prevent stabilization or healing. [SRD p. 98, Stabilizing a Creature](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md) · [SRD p. 86, Suffocating](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

<a id="srd51-core-monsters-and-death"></a>
### Monsters and death

`srd51.core.monsters-and-death`

Most GMs let ordinary monsters die immediately when they reach 0 hit points. The GM can have a major villain or another exceptional nonplayer character fall unconscious and use the same 0-hit-point rules as a player character. [SRD p. 98, Monsters and Death](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md)

<a id="srd51-core-knocking-a-creature-out"></a>
### Knocking a creature out

`srd51.core.knocking-a-creature-out`

When your melee attack reduces a creature to 0 hit points, you may choose at the instant you deal the damage to leave it unconscious and stable. This choice is tied to reducing the creature to 0 with a melee attack. A ranged attack or effect without a melee attack cannot use this option. [SRD p. 98, Knocking a Creature Out](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md)

**Project interpretation.** Apply the melee knockout as the specific outcome when chosen, even when an ordinary monster would die at 0 or excess melee damage would otherwise qualify for instant death. The knockout grants the choice on reducing the creature to 0 with no separate damage cap. An alternate reading resolves instant death first and permits a knockout only for a surviving creature. [SRD p. 98, Instant Death, Monsters and Death, and Knocking a Creature Out](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md)

<a id="srd51-core-temporary-hit-points"></a>
## Temporary hit points

`srd51.core.temporary-hit-points`

Track granted temporary hit points separately from actual hit points. They absorb incoming damage first and any excess then reduces actual hit points. Temporary hit points can exist when the creature is at full health or at 0, and the pool can exceed the ordinary hit point maximum. Healing cannot refill the pool. When another grant arrives, the recipient chooses the existing pool or the new one. Do not add the pools. A pool with no specified duration lasts until depleted or the creature finishes a long rest. A grant with a duration uses that duration and can be depleted earlier. [SRD pp. 98–99, Temporary Hit Points](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md) · [SRD p. 99, Temporary Hit Points continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-099.md)

Temporary hit points at 0 can absorb damage but do not stabilize or wake the creature. **Project interpretation.** For a trigger that says the creature takes damage, including [concentration](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-concentration) and damage while already at 0, use the amount after damage modifiers and before spending the temporary buffer. The source describes the creature taking damage and then losing temporary hit points first. An alternative reading counts only the spillover for these triggers. A trigger that expressly requires losing actual hit points uses the spillover instead. [SRD pp. 98–99, Temporary Hit Points and Damage at 0 Hit Points](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md) · [SRD p. 99, Temporary Hit Points continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-099.md) · [SRD p. 102, Concentration](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-102.md)

**Acceptance cases.** A creature with 4 temporary hit points taking 9 damage loses the pool and 5 actual hit points. A creature with 8 temporary hit points offered 5 can retain 8 or replace them with 5. Under the documented trigger reading, a concentrating caster who takes 24 damage all absorbed by temporary hit points still makes a DC 12 Constitution save. [SRD p. 98, Temporary Hit Points](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-098.md) · [SRD p. 102, Concentration](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-102.md)

<a id="srd51-core-mounted-combat"></a>
## Mounted combat

`srd51.core.mounted-combat`

A mount must be willing, at least one size category larger than the rider, and suitably built for riding. Once during your move, you can mount a creature within 5 feet or dismount. Spend movement equal to half your speed. You need that much movement remaining and a speed above 0. If your mount is moved against its will while you are riding, or if you are knocked prone while mounted, make a DC 10 Dexterity save. Failure dismounts you and you land prone in a space within 5 feet of the mount. If the mount itself is knocked prone, you can instead spend your reaction to dismount as it falls and land on your feet. Without that reaction you dismount and fall prone within 5 feet. [SRD p. 99, Mounted Combat and Mounting and Dismounting](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-099.md)

<a id="srd51-core-controlling-a-mount"></a>
### Controlling a mount

`srd51.core.controlling-a-mount`

The rider can control a mount trained to accept riders or let it act independently. Domestic riding animals are assumed trained. Intelligent creatures act independently. When mounted, a controlled mount's initiative changes to match the rider's. It can move and act even on the turn it is mounted. It moves as the rider directs and its action options are limited to Dash, Disengage, and Dodge. An independent mount keeps its initiative and uses its ordinary movement and actions according to its own decisions. If either kind of mount provokes an opportunity attack while ridden, the attacker can target the mount or rider. [SRD p. 99, Controlling a Mount](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-099.md)

**Project interpretation.** Use distinct rider and mount turns at the same initiative, with the ordinary GM and player decisions for ties. The source matches initiatives and preserves the mount's ability to act when mounted, but does not describe interleaving or an extra turn. If the rider mounts during their turn and the mount still has its turn for the round, the mount can take it at the shared initiative immediately after the rider if necessary. Moving a mount's initiative never grants it a second turn in that round. The alternative is to interleave the mount's movement and action with the rider's turn, but that would require a procedure that p. 99 does not give. [SRD p. 90, The Order of Combat and Initiative](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-090.md) · [SRD p. 99, Controlling a Mount](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-099.md)

<a id="srd51-core-underwater-combat"></a>
## Underwater combat

`srd51.core.underwater-combat`

Resolve three independent underwater predicates. For **fire damage**, full immersion gives resistance to a creature or object. For a **ranged weapon attack**, a target farther than normal range is an automatic miss. Within that range, apply disadvantage except to a crossbow, a net, or a weapon thrown like a javelin, including darts, spears, and tridents. For a **melee weapon attack**, a swimming speed, whether natural or magical, avoids the water penalty. Without such a speed, apply disadvantage unless using a dagger, javelin, shortsword, spear, or trident. [SRD p. 99, Underwater Combat](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-099.md)

**Acceptance cases.** Without a swimming speed, a spear thrust underwater avoids the environment's disadvantage but an axe attack does not. A crossbow avoids the weapon disadvantage at normal range but still automatically misses beyond it. A mount knocked prone lets its rider use a reaction to land standing, while a rider who already used the reaction is dismounted and lands prone. [SRD p. 99, Underwater Combat and Mounting and Dismounting](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-099.md) · [SRD p. 91, Reactions](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md)
