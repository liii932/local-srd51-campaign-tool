# Exploration, rest, and downtime

<a id="srd51-core-time"></a>
## Time

`srd51.core.time`

The GM sets elapsed time for tasks when timing matters. Minutes fit local exploration, hours fit many wilderness or urban journeys, and days fit long journeys. Combat and other fast-moving sequences use approximately six-second rounds. The GM may narrate movement and arrival without tracking exact distance or duration, including travel through large indoor sites between encounters. When precise travel matters, use speed, pace, and terrain. [SRD p. 84, Time and Movement](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-084.md)

<a id="srd51-core-day-boundary"></a>
### Day boundary

`srd51.core.day-boundary`

**Project interpretation.** For a recurring per-day allowance or trigger without its own reset, use one in-world calendar day. The next campaign day starts the next allowance. The GM establishes the applicable calendar and boundary if needed. An alternative is a rolling 24 hours since the last occurrence. This interpretation preserves the recurring wording of provisions such as Arcane Recovery and Geas. For an effect that lasts for one day, count 24 hours of elapsed time from its commencement. Its duration describes a span of time, so midnight alone does not end it. A specified dawn, dusk, rest, elapsed 24 hours, or other reset takes precedence. The monster stat-block notation [X/Day](creatures.md#srd51-creature-rules-limited-usage) specifically resets by long rest. [SRD p. 84, Time](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-084.md) · [SRD p. 53, Arcane Recovery](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-053.md) · [SRD p. 87, Long Rest](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md) · [SRD p. 148, Geas](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-148.md) · [SRD p. 259, Limited Usage](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-259.md)

<a id="srd51-core-speed"></a>
## Speed

`srd51.core.speed`

A creature's walking speed is the distance in feet it can walk in a round of energetic movement, before movement costs or modifications. For longer travel, use the applicable pace rather than extending the short-burst speed for hours. [Combat movement](combat.md#srd51-core-combat-movement) handles movement spent within a turn and switching between different speeds. [SRD p. 84, Speed and Travel Pace](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-084.md) · [SRD p. 91, Movement and Position](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md)

<a id="srd51-core-travel-pace"></a>
## Travel pace

`srd51.core.travel-pace`

When using travel rates, choose a pace for the party. Per-minute distances are in feet. Hourly and daily distances are in miles, with daily distances based on eight hours of travel. The daily rates are their own values. [SRD p. 84, Travel Pace](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-084.md)

- **Normal:** 300 feet per minute, 3 miles per hour, or 24 miles per day.
- **Slow:** 200 feet per minute, 2 miles per hour, or 18 miles per day. A party can use stealth and search an area more carefully at this pace.
- **Fast:** 400 feet per minute, 4 miles per hour, or 30 miles per day. Apply a −5 penalty to [passive Wisdom (Perception)](abilities.md#srd51-core-passive-check) scores.

<a id="srd51-core-forced-march"></a>
### Forced March

`srd51.core.forced-march`

The party may travel past eight hours in a day. For each additional hour, use the hourly distance for the chosen pace and have every traveling character make a Constitution save at the end of the hour. The DC is 10 plus the number of hours beyond eight, so the ninth hour is DC 11 and the tenth DC 12. Each failure adds one level of [exhaustion](conditions.md#srd51-condition-exhaustion). Use the creature's current penalties on each successive save, including disadvantage if exhaustion has reached level 3. [SRD p. 84, Forced March](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-084.md) · [SRD p. 358, Exhaustion](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-358.md)

<a id="srd51-core-travel-mounts-and-vehicles"></a>
### Mounts and vehicles

`srd51.core.travel-mounts-and-vehicles`

A mounted character can gallop for about an hour, covering twice the usual fast-pace distance for that span. Fresh mounts available every 8–10 miles can extend this rate, an unusual arrangement outside densely inhabited areas. Land vehicles use the normal choices of pace. Waterborne travel instead uses the vessel's speed and omits fast-pace penalties and slow-pace benefits. A ship can potentially operate for up to 24 hours per day depending on vessel and crew. Particular mounts or vehicles can grant faster travel. [SRD p. 84, Mounts and Vehicles](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-084.md)

<a id="srd51-core-travel-difficult-terrain"></a>
### Difficult terrain

`srd51.core.travel-difficult-terrain`

For difficult terrain, each foot crossed costs two feet of movement. A party spends the same time to cover half the listed distance. Dense forest, rubble, deep swamp, steep mountains, or ice can count as difficult terrain. For example, a full hour at normal pace in difficult terrain covers 1.5 miles. [SRD p. 85, Difficult Terrain](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-085.md)

<a id="srd51-core-special-movement"></a>
## Special movement

`srd51.core.special-movement`

For climbing or swimming without the corresponding speed, spend two feet of movement per foot traveled, or three feet in difficult terrain. A matching speed removes the extra climbing or swimming cost. Difficult terrain still adds its own movement cost under the general terrain rule. The GM can require a successful Strength (Athletics) check to climb a slippery or nearly holdless surface, or to make any progress in rough water. The heading also names crawling. The explicit crawling cost is in [prone movement](combat.md#srd51-core-being-prone). [SRD p. 85, Difficult Terrain and Climbing, Swimming, and Crawling](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-085.md) · [SRD p. 92, Being Prone continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-092.md)

<a id="srd51-core-jumping"></a>
### Jumping

`srd51.core.jumping`

For either jump, a running start requires at least 10 feet traveled on foot immediately before takeoff. Each foot cleared in a jump costs a foot of movement, in addition to the run-up. A running long jump covers up to your Strength score in feet. A standing long jump covers up to half that distance. The baseline assumes vertical clearance is immaterial. The GM may call for a DC 10 Strength (Athletics) check to clear an obstacle at most one-quarter as high as the length of the jump. On failure you hit the obstacle. A landing in difficult terrain requires a DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check to remain on your feet. Failure lands you [prone](conditions.md#srd51-condition-prone). [SRD p. 85, Long Jump](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-085.md)

The ordinary height of a running high jump is 3 feet plus your Strength modifier. If this amount is zero or lower, the formula grants no positive height. A standing high jump reaches half the running height. The GM may allow a Strength (Athletics) check to exceed the ordinary height. With arms extended during a jump, the highest point you can reach is the jump height plus 1.5 times your own height. [SRD p. 85, High Jump](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-085.md)

**Acceptance cases.** With Strength 16 and 30 feet of movement, walking 10 feet immediately before takeoff permits a 16-foot long jump and leaves 4 feet afterward. With 12 feet left after the run-up, the character cannot cover 16 feet using that movement. If the 16-foot jump crosses a 4-foot barrier, the GM may require the DC 10 obstacle check. Landing in rubble requires the separate landing check. [SRD p. 85, Long Jump](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-085.md)

<a id="srd51-core-falling"></a>
## Falling

`srd51.core.falling`

When the fall ends, roll 1d6 bludgeoning damage per complete 10 feet fallen, up to 20d6. Land prone if the fall deals damage. A creature that avoids all of the falling damage avoids being made prone by this rule. A 240-foot fall still uses 20d6. [SRD p. 86, Falling](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

<a id="srd51-core-suffocating"></a>
## Suffocating

`srd51.core.suffocating`

Track two stages using the creature's Constitution modifier. The initial breath reserve lasts the greater of 30 seconds or (1 + modifier) minutes. Once that reserve runs out, or immediately on choking, count the greater of one round or the modifier in rounds. At the first start of the creature's turn after that count, set its hit points to 0 and make it dying. While it still cannot breathe, prevent all healing and stabilization. A modifier of −2 yields a 30-second reserve followed by a one-round grace period. [SRD p. 86, Suffocating](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

<a id="srd51-core-vision-and-light"></a>
## Vision and light

`srd51.core.vision-and-light`

Bright light lets most creatures see normally, including ordinary daylight on overcast days and artificial illumination within its radius. Dim light, such as twilight, often lies between bright light and darkness and is lightly obscured. Patchy fog and sparse vegetation can also lightly obscure an area. Apply disadvantage to Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight in such an area. Darkness, including magical darkness, dense vegetation, or opaque fog can heavily obscure an area. Vision is blocked and a creature trying to see a target in that area effectively suffers [blinded](conditions.md#srd51-condition-blinded) for that attempt. Resolve separately any sensory rule that lets it perceive the target. [SRD p. 86, Vision and Light](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

<a id="srd51-core-blindsight"></a>
### Blindsight

`srd51.core.blindsight`

Within its specified radius, a creature with blindsight perceives its surroundings without using sight. The creature's entry supplies its radius and any limitations. [SRD p. 86, Blindsight](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

<a id="srd51-core-darkvision"></a>
### Darkvision

`srd51.core.darkvision`

Within its stated range, darkvision treats darkness as dim light for the creature. Darkness consequently counts as lightly obscured for that creature, and it sees only in grayscale there. Magical effects can prevent ordinary darkvision from seeing through their darkness. A monster with darkvision also treats dim light as bright light within the radius stated in its stat block. [SRD p. 86, Darkvision](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md) · [SRD p. 133, Darkness](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-133.md) · [SRD p. 257, Monster Darkvision](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-257.md)

<a id="srd51-core-racial-darkvision"></a>
#### Ancestry Darkvision

`srd51.core.racial-darkvision`

The dwarf, elf, gnome, half-elf, half-orc, and tiefling Darkvision traits use a 60-foot range. Within that range, treat dim light as bright light and darkness as dim light. The creature sees only shades of gray in darkness. An elf viewing an object 60 feet away in dim light therefore avoids the dim-light penalty for a sight-based Wisdom (Perception) check. If that object is instead in darkness, the elf treats it as dimly lit and the penalty applies. [SRD p. 3, Dwarf Darkvision](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-003.md) · [SRD p. 4, Elf Darkvision](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-004.md) · [SRD p. 6, Gnome Darkvision](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-006.md) · [SRD p. 7, Half-Elf, Half-Orc, and Tiefling Darkvision](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-007.md) · [SRD p. 86, Vision and Light](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

<a id="srd51-core-truesight"></a>
### Truesight

`srd51.core.truesight`

Within the specified range, truesight sees through normal and magical darkness, sees invisible creatures and objects, automatically detects visual illusions and succeeds on saves against them, sees the original form of shapechangers and magically transformed creatures, and sees into the Ethereal Plane. [SRD p. 86, Truesight](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

**Acceptance case.** In ordinary darkness within darkvision range, a guard can see monochrome detail and has disadvantage on a check that depends on sight. Listening to footsteps does not incur that sight-based penalty. A visual illusion within truesight range is automatically detected, and the truesight creature automatically succeeds on a save against it. [SRD p. 86, Vision and Light, Darkvision, and Truesight](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md)

<a id="srd51-core-food-and-water"></a>
## Food and water

`srd51.core.food-and-water`

Exhaustion from food or water deprivation cannot be removed until the character eats and drinks the full required amounts. This restriction applies even when an [exhaustion recovery rule](conditions.md#srd51-condition-exhaustion) otherwise requires only some food and drink. [SRD pp. 86–87, Food and Water](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md) · [SRD p. 87, Food and Water continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md) · [SRD p. 358, Exhaustion](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-358.md)

<a id="srd51-core-food"></a>
### Food

`srd51.core.food`

A character needs one pound of food per day. A half-pound ration counts as half a day without food. A full day of ordinary eating resets the deprivation count to zero. A character can tolerate 3 plus the Constitution modifier days without food, with a minimum of one day. Each day that ends with the accumulated deprivation beyond that limit adds one exhaustion level without a save. For example, with Constitution modifier +0, six consecutive half-ration days reach the three-day limit, and a seventh half-ration day exceeds it. [SRD p. 86, Food](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md) · [SRD p. 87, Food continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md)

**Project interpretation.** For rations below one pound, add the fraction of the one-pound requirement that the character did not eat to the days-without-food count. This extends the stated 0, half, and full ration outcomes proportionally, with a full ration still resetting the count. The source expressly describes full and half rations and supplies no rule for other partial amounts. An alternative is to let the GM classify unusual amounts into the stated ration bands. [SRD p. 86, Food](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-086.md) · [SRD p. 87, Food continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md)

<a id="srd51-core-water"></a>
### Water

`srd51.core.water`

A character needs one gallon daily, or two gallons in hot weather. If the character drinks half the requirement, a DC 15 Constitution save at the end of the day prevents water-deprivation exhaustion on success. Below half, the exhaustion occurs automatically. Whenever either branch imposes exhaustion, add one level to a character with none or two levels to a character who already has at least one. [SRD p. 87, Water](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md)

**Project interpretation.** The source expressly covers full water, exactly half, and less than half. Apply the DC 15 branch to an incomplete ration of at least half and below full. This preserves the full requirement and puts any amount greater than half in the least severe specified shortage branch. The source supplies no additional penalty for a separate intermediate tier. In hot weather, one gallon requires the save and three quarters of a gallon imposes exhaustion automatically. [SRD p. 87, Water](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md)

<a id="srd51-core-interacting-with-objects"></a>
## Interacting with objects

`srd51.core.interacting-with-objects`

The player states the intended manipulation and the GM resolves what it does. For a difficult manipulation or an attempt to break an object by force, the GM may set a DC for a Strength check. Weapon and spell damage can affect objects subject to targeting and the object's traits. Objects are immune to poison and psychic damage, automatically fail Strength and Dexterity saves, and are immune to effects requiring saves of other abilities. The GM sets AC and hit points and can assign resistance or immunity according to the object and type of attack. At 0 hit points the object breaks. [Combat interaction](combat.md#srd51-core-other-activity-on-your-turn) governs when an interaction uses an action. [SRD p. 87, Interacting with Objects](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md) · [SRD p. 91, Other Activity on Your Turn](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-091.md)

<a id="srd51-core-short-rest"></a>
## Short rest

`srd51.core.short-rest`

A short rest requires at least one hour of downtime with activity no more strenuous than eating, drinking, reading, or tending wounds. At its end the player can spend available Hit Dice up to the character's total, which equals character level. For each die spent, roll it and add the character's Constitution modifier to calculate healing. The player can decide after each result whether to spend another available die. Apply the ordinary hit point maximum to healing. A 59-minute pause gives no short rest benefits. A 4th-level character with only two unspent Hit Dice can spend at most two, choosing whether to spend the second after seeing the first result. [SRD p. 87, Short Rest](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md) · [SRD p. 97, Healing](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-097.md)

**Project interpretation.** Treat a negative Hit Die healing total as zero. The text describes regained hit points and gives no operation that deals damage on a low roll. The die is spent even if the total grants no healing. [SRD p. 87, Short Rest](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md)

<a id="srd51-core-long-rest"></a>
## Long rest

`srd51.core.long-rest`

A long rest requires at least eight hours of downtime, with sleeping or light activities such as reading, talking, eating, or standing watch. Standing watch is limited to two hours. If a period of strenuous adventuring activity lasting at least one hour interrupts the rest, start the rest over to obtain benefits. A creature must have at least 1 hit point when it starts the rest and can benefit from only one long rest in a 24-hour period. [SRD p. 87, Long Rest](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md)

**Project interpretation.** Read the one-hour threshold as applying to the strenuous period as a whole, including walking, fighting, casting, or similar activities within it. The list describes the contents of the period, so a short fight on its own does not force a restart under this reading. Read the two-hour limit as applying to standing watch, the activity the source directly qualifies. The local wording can also be read as placing a two-hour cap on all light activity. It gives no separate minimum number of hours of sleep. [SRD p. 87, Long Rest](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md)

On qualifying completion, regain all lost hit points and regain spent Hit Dice up to half your total Hit Dice, with a minimum recovery limit of one die. Recovery cannot exceed the number spent. For odd totals, the greatest whole count within the half-total cap is the limit, subject to the stated minimum. The rest also restores [spell slots](spellcasting.md#srd51-core-spell-slots), expires ordinary [temporary hit points](combat.md#srd51-core-temporary-hit-points), and reduces [exhaustion](conditions.md#srd51-condition-exhaustion) when its food and drink requirement is met. Individual features can have their own short- or long-rest recovery. [SRD p. 87, Long Rest](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md) · [SRD p. 99, Temporary Hit Points continuation](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-099.md) · [SRD p. 100, Spell Slots](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-100.md) · [SRD p. 358, Exhaustion](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-358.md)

**Acceptance cases.** A 5th-level character who has spent all five Hit Dice and completes an eligible long rest regains two. A character who starts the rest at 0 hit points cannot qualify for that rest's benefits. A second completed eight-hour pause inside the same 24-hour period gives no second set of long-rest benefits. [SRD p. 87, Long Rest](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-087.md)

<a id="srd51-core-between-adventures"></a>
## Between adventures

`srd51.core.between-adventures`

The GM can advance between-adventure time by declaration and invite a broad description of characters' activities, or track elapsed time while other events proceed. Each character chooses and pays for a lifestyle. Lifestyle can influence how people and groups respond. Its effect on a particular social encounter depends on the situation. [SRD p. 88, Between Adventures and Lifestyle Expenses](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-088.md)

<a id="srd51-core-downtime-activities"></a>
## Downtime activities

`srd51.core.downtime-activities`

Each activity requires the applicable number of days before it yields its benefit. A day counts when you spend at least eight hours of it on that activity. Counted days can be nonconsecutive. Characters with more time can continue or change activities. Other activities are possible by arrangement with the GM. [SRD p. 88, Downtime Activities](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-088.md)

<a id="srd51-core-crafting"></a>
### Crafting

`srd51.core.crafting`

To craft nonmagical equipment, art, or another object, have proficiency with the appropriate tools and access to any required materials or facilities. Each counted day yields up to 5 gp of total market value across one or more items, or advances a higher-value item by 5 gp until complete. The raw materials cost half the completed items' market value. Contributors to one item must all have the required proficiency and work together at the same place. Each contributes 5 gp of progress per day. While crafting, a character can maintain a modest lifestyle free of its ordinary 1 gp daily charge or a comfortable lifestyle at half its ordinary cost. A 20 gp item takes four solo days and 10 gp in materials. Two eligible contributors working together complete it in two days at the same material cost. [SRD p. 88, Crafting](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-088.md)

<a id="srd51-core-practicing-a-profession"></a>
### Practicing a profession

`srd51.core.practicing-a-profession`

While working at a profession between adventures, a character can maintain a modest lifestyle without paying its 1 gp daily expense. A member of an organization able to provide gainful employment can instead maintain a comfortable lifestyle. A character who is proficient in Performance and uses it in downtime can instead maintain a wealthy lifestyle. These benefits continue while the qualifying work continues. [SRD p. 88, Practicing a Profession](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-088.md)

<a id="srd51-core-recuperating"></a>
### Recuperating

`srd51.core.recuperating`

After three counted days recovering from injury, disease, or poison, the character can attempt a DC 15 Constitution save. On success choose either to end one effect on the character that prevents regaining hit points or to gain 24 hours of advantage on saves against one currently affecting disease or poison. A failed save grants neither benefit. [SRD p. 88, Recuperating](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-088.md)

<a id="srd51-core-researching"></a>
### Researching

`srd51.core.researching`

When research begins, the GM decides whether the information is available, the required downtime days, and any restrictions such as locating a particular source or person. The GM can require one or more ability checks, such as Investigation to find leads or Persuasion to obtain help. Once the requirements are met, the character learns the available information. Pay 1 gp per day of research in addition to ordinary lifestyle expenses. [SRD p. 89, Researching](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-089.md)

<a id="srd51-core-training"></a>
### Training

`srd51.core.training`

Find a willing instructor to learn a new language or gain proficiency with a set of tools. The stated training period is 250 days, costing 1 gp per day. The GM adjudicates the time needed and whether checks are required, and can allow other training options. Gain the language or tool proficiency only after satisfying the required time and cost. A partial schedule by itself grants no proficiency. [SRD p. 89, Training](../../references/srd-5.1/pages/page-089.md)
